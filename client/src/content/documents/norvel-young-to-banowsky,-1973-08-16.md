This is a letter from Pepperdine chancellor [[M. Norvel Young]] to president [[William S. Banowsky]], dated August 16, 1973. In the letter, Young advises Banowsky not to accept a job offer to become president of Lubbock Christian College.

>\[handwritten\] Confidential
>August 16, 1973
>
>President Banowsky
>Dr. Young
>
>It’s 6:45 A.M., August 16th. I would like to react to the questions you put me last night.
>1. As your friend, I still have the gut feeling that you should do what is best for your own soul and your own family. “To thine own self be true. It follows as night follows the day that thou canst be false to no man.” You need to be well grounded and if you are not, no matter what your achievements, you will not find real happiness. I am not sure that this means that you will find yourself if you are in another geographical setting and still “possessed” with the desire to be elected to a high political office. I think you will probably find that politics is just as dirty and bemeaning \[*sic*\] in one part of the country as in another. However, it may be that the ethos of Texas is more akin to your own. I think there is no doubt that Texas tends to be more consistent with our moral and cultural values than California, though it is moving rapidly. As you would seek to appeal to a wide segment of voters, particularly in the urban areas, you would find it difficult to keep a lot of the church people behind you. You would have a base but also you would have more troubles and opposition from your own “family”. I don’t feel too qualified to judge where your best base would be from a political point of view, but I can understand that you would be more at home in Texas. On the other hand, I think there must be many more viable candidates who are similar to you in Texas than there are such candidates in California. It is certainly a gamble to give up the position which you have here on the assumption that you can gain a similar position in two or three years there. You would be in a better position to evaluate the risk involved. I think the risks are worth it politically if you feel that you need to make this move to be well grounded culturally and spiritually and to give your children this kind of grounding. At the risk of sounding pious, I can’t help but think of the words “What shall a man be profited if he gain the whole ‘state’ and lose his own soul”. As your friend, I think I must vote for you and your family and let the other risks take care of themselves. For example, you may be like a beautiful girl who has a wonderful voice who could become a famous opera singer or movie star but she realizes that she can’t do this without giving up her husband and her children. So, she decides not to go to Italy to study or to Hollywood to act. Later on, she may look back with some regrets when she thinks what might have been, but I think she can live with those regrets better than she can live with the regrets of a broken home or of wayward children. In a way, maybe life is like that. You have to choose the kind of regrets you want to live with.
>2. Now we come to timing. This is very important from the standpoint of the University, and I think it is important from the standpoint of your own sense of accomplishment and self-worth. I know that there will never be a good time, but I believe that now is especially a bad time for you to make this move. If you decide that you should go to Texas, I believe you should allow at least nine to twelve months to make the move in a clean, professional manner, not impetuously and not with a lot of loose ends. I know this would be very difficult for you and that you would like to move quickly, but I think when you look back you will be glad you took time to do it right. This Malibu campus is not really complete without a Fine Arts Building and an Administration Building. It is in a hyphenated state - an unfinished state. We also, of course, need some married housing but that is not as urgent and could be done with borrowed funds. No one knows better than you how serious our cash crunch is, and with interest rates as they are, it is exceedingly serious to not be able to pay down the amount we owe the Bank of America January 1 to the amount that we can handle on a three per cent long term loan. Working together, I believe we can get prospects to commit and we can get plans approved, and I think within six to nine months we can see this campus on the way to completion, even though the buildings will obviously not be completed, and we can see the financing in a viable shape. Then we are facing opposition and threatened law suits from some quarters. I am frankly not concerned about the Osom \[?\] case except for minor harrassment \[*sic*\] of our donors and some possible publicity, but I am concerned about the [[Trent Devenney affair|Devenney matter]] and what he might stir up from the standpoint of publicity. I think any announcement of your making a change would be misinterpreted and that this would not be good for the institution at this point. I don’t think you would feel good about it even if you were in Texas. Texas and California are getting closer every day with communications and travel. You are always going to have friends here and be interested in them even if you change your base of operations. I think it is important to handle this matter efficiently. In the time frame in which I am speaking, you and I would have time to select and prepare a successor to carry on. This would give you a sense of satisfaction just as it does to see [[Joe Barnett]] do a good job at [[Broadway Church of Christ|Broadway]]. But, this would take some effort and energy. It can’t be done while you are packing. I know that [[Lubbock Christian University|Lubbock Christian]] needs you yesterday. On the other hand, however, if they had the assurance that you were coming, they could do quite a bit in anticipation of that time and even you could work with them in a pre-inauguration period, making certain changes and preparations for your coming. I realize you are concerned about your boys, but I think they could be put into a Christian school in a holding pattern in anticipation of your change for one year. The school year isn’t a very long time and it seems shorter all the time. The satisfaction of a job well done and properly wrapped up will be with you for a lifetime. Think about this.
