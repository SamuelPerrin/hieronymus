This is a memo to file, written by Pepperdine executive vice president [[Howard A. White]], dated February 13, 1974. In the memo, White recounts the first Tyler Ecology Award dinner and a conversation he had with chancellor [[M. Norvel Young]] concerning president [[William S. Banowsky]]’s future.

>February 13, 1974
>
>On Friday, February 6, we had the [[Tyler Ecology Award dinner]] at the [[Beverly Wilshire Hotel]], to give out the $150,000 in prizes that were provided by the %5-million \[*sic*\] gift from the [[Alice Tyler|Tuler]] \[*sic*] family. This year the award went to three distinguished scientists for their work during the past ten years in preserving the environment. The [[Patrons of Pepperdine]], a group of social leaders organized by [[Alice Tyler|Mrs. Tyler]], planned much of the program.
>
>It was a glittering evening, with many celebrities present, including [[Gene Autry]], Irene Dunne, Conrad Hilton, and [[Ronald Reagan|Governor]] and [[Nancy Reagan|Mrs. Reagan]]. I sat at the table next to Governor Reagan and with Dr. and Mrs. [[Glenn S. Dumke|Glenn Dumke]] (he is Chancellor the \[*sic*\] California State Colleges and Universities). Mrs. Tyler wore a necklace reputed to be worth more than $500,000. There was champagne on the tables and dancing followed the dinner.
>
>Of course both the dancing and the champagne would displease many of our church leaders. Both were planned by the support group, but of course with considerable cooperation from [[William S. Banowsky|Bill]] and [[Gay Banowsky|Gay]]. [[M. Norvel Young|Norvel]] and [[Helen Mattox Young|Helen]] would like to have had it otherwise, but were powerless to do anything.
>
>So today I spent considerable time talking on the phone and in person to various ones, trying to guide what appears in our student newspapers at Malibu and Los Angeles so that we would not look worse than we had to to the students, to whom we are a consummate bunch of hypocrites for forbidding dancing on campus (and alcohol) and having such at the dinner. ([[Helen Pepperdine|Mrs. Pepperdine]] refused to attend the dinner because of the dancing that was to follow).
>
>So far as I know, only Bill and Gay among the Pepperdiners stayed to dance, and Pepperdiners generally did not drink their champagne.
>
>It is very clear that we are in a state of great tension between our church affiliation and the pressures of the people with power and money who want us to conform to their life styles. \[*sic*]
>*****
> Today Norvel called to say that he had just had a long talk with Bill, who had just returned from Atlanta, and that Bill told him that he (Bill) has thought things over, is sorry he ever took the stand he did on dancing, that he wants to get closer to Christ, and that he is finished with politics. He claims he would like to broaden Pepperdine and add to its board some evangelicals who are not members of the [[Churches of Christ|Church of Christ]], and that if he cannot do that, he would probably accept [[Banowsky and the LCC job|the offer to be president]] of [[Lubbock Christian University|Lubbock Christian College]]. ([[Jerry Hudson]] reported some time ago that Bill said on a scale of one to ten, being provost at Maliby \[*sic*] is more important than being president at Lubbock). But Norvel said Bill had convinced Norvel that Bill was sincere.
> 
> If it were not so serious, it would be very tiresome to hear this same old line with little variation. If Pepperdine is now too narrow to please Bill, it is silly to talk about returning to Lubbock where the prevailing opinions are much more conservative than Pepperdine. I pointed this out to Norvel and told him that Bill will no more return to Lubbock than I will open a house of prostitution. I told Norvel that this is one more ploy to break Norvel down and get him to agree to the moves Bill wants to make to turn Pepperdine into a better platform from which to launch a political campagin \[*sic*\] in California. Norvel said Bill inquired if Norvel would like to return to Lubbock as president of Lubbock Christian, which of course Norvel doses \[*sic*\] not wish to do. This is good indication that Bill is playing games. If he were to go to Texas, it would not be two months before he would be in politics there.
> 
> So now it remains to be seen what move Bill will make next, but I am sure he will keep up the pressure on Norvel to separate Pepperdine from its church heritage.
