This is a letter from Pepperdine president [[William S. Banowsky]] to [[David Reagan]], dated November 19, 1973. In the letter, Banowsky apologizes that Pepperdine doesn’t have a job for David Reagan and discusses the conflict he feels over whether to accept a job offer from Lubbock Christian College.

>November 19, 1973
>
>Dear David:
>It is true that our recent letters crossed in the mail. To put the finishing touches on, let me say that I very much appreciate your position and understand it completely. I am sorry that neither of the positions we presently have open appeals to you. If we have future openings, I will certainly be in touch. You and Ann made a great impression on all our people.
>
>You certainly were not overly candid in making your remarks about Pepperdine University’s philosophical perplexities. I hope you can appreciate the fact that we have grown to the very edge of the brotherhood, and now stand with one foot in and one out. And this not only gives us our greatness, but some schizophrenia. Your analysis of our situation perfectly describes some of my own frustrations and it is precisely the frustration that causes me to wonder whether I wish to be propelled into a more consistent posture by moving outward in the field of politics or turn back into a more consistent posture by moving once again into the brotherhood circles. That is really what the [[Banowsky and the LCC job|Lubbock decision]] was all about.
>
>We appreciate your friendship. [[Gay Banowsky|Gay]] joins me in sending our love to Ann.
>
>Sincerely,
>William S. Banowsky
