This is a letter from Pepperdine president [[William S. Banowsky]] to chancellor [[M. Norvel Young]], dated August 19, 1973. In the letter, Banowsky offers to stay at Pepperdine for seven years if Young agrees to [[the opening of the board|open]] the university’s [[Board of Trustees]] to nonmembers of the Churches of Christ.

>HANDWRITTEN NOTE FROM WSB TO MNY Aug 19, 1973
>I. Regret the meeting. [[Robert Jones|Bob Jones]] got rough and put it on the basis of duty and responsibility, hence I felt compelled to point out what I think is the _basic_ irresponsibility of the [[Board of Trustees|Board]]. I find it strange that, on the one hand he says the school is and will go under, and yet he refuses to extend responsibility for its survival to those who could see it through financially. In fact, I think it is fundamentally selfish of Bob to confess, as he did in the Board meeting, that five years ago he was all for stepping aside and going with a Board of Governors because there was no other hope, but now that you and I have seen it through to this point, he doesn’t want to consider loss of control. He really can’t have it both ways! If he genuinely feels that the school can’t make it now, he can’t say that, because of our greater strength vis a vis 1968, we should forego the Board o- \[*sic*\] Governors.
>
>II. I can _only_ be effective when I can be whole hearted and completely clear as to what we are doing. Otherwise the drive and motive power goes out of me. You may call this my “either-or syndrome” or whatever you wish, but at rock bottom I have to sense a basic symetry \[*sic*\], and a fundamental philosophical consistency to what I am doing--If I am to focus and unleash my usual energies.
>
>I know you find it very strange that on the sone \[*sic*\] hand I talk of solving our problems at Pepperdine by moving toward a more secular stance while, at the same time plunging myself deeply back into the parochialism and sectarianism of the brotherhood.
>But the Lubbock options, in addition to answering my emotional needs, answer this very basic need for philosophical consistency. I am sure that, while there will be many problems, I can make peace with the _basic direction_, and the problems will, at least, be those of my own heritage.
>
>Pepperdine is in another world altogether. The church-relatedness is impossible, in a meaningful and long range sense. And much of the problem is, that, in addition to my need for basic concictency \[*sic*\] and direction, I also have a need to feel that the work has genuine long range integrity. We don’t have the immediate rewards of the faculty, or of the minister--we can only keep driving because we feel that what we are building will last--that it is solidly founded--that it can endure the storms. That’s the very urge I had when trying to decide whether to build our academic buildings out os \[*sic*\] stucco and studs--like the dormitories--or whether to do it more solidly and _permanently_ for the long range view. The two or three million extra which we spent will prove to be a very wise decision.
>
>I _think_, notice I said _think_, I could make a very great, long-term commitment to Pepperdine by going for academic excellence. This would answer that half of me which is pulled in that directions \[*sic*\]. It would be faiathful \[*sic*\] to my deepest sense of intelligence, and I _think_ I could gain deeply solid philosophical motive power for the gruelling \[*sic*\] work to be done by aligning my heart and emotions, as well as my mind with the wonderfully rich tradition of higher education. Along with the religious pursuit, which is essentially an emotional reality, the pursuit of truth through learning is one of mankind’s highest and most sacred and ancient traditions. Aside from the home itself and religious institutions, British and European schools are the oldest institutions on earth. Harvard is 110 years older than the United States government. I guess one of our basic differences might be our different capacity to have real respect for what [[Oxford]], or [[Harvard]], or [[Vanderbilt]], or L. A. State or [[USC|U. S. C.]] can and have meant to mankind.
>
>I think higher education is one of our greatest hopes. I know that it is possible to feel only contempt for such institutions because of the changing details of life style and more especially because of their _essential open_ and free spirit of inquiry. But Lubbock or not, I will never again feel basic contempt. I believe I could find deep satisfaction in helping this campus, which we have created, to take its place in the rich tradition of that open spirit. But I AM NOT NAIVE to think that once we cross that center line--wherever it is--that we could ever again control the outcome. After all, open is open, and closed is closed, and we can’t have it both ways. I also see that to add one nonchurch member to the [[Board of Trustees|Board]] would be the beginning of the end for the closed system, and hence, if one, we might as well add twenty.
>
>But what I also feel is that, within a matter of 5 to 15 years, the closed system will go anyway. That is, we will be dragged, kicking and screaming across that center line. [[Howard A. White|Howard]] confessed much last night when he said that our age differences was a big point in what we were willing to risk.
>
>I just _can’t_ make the commitment now that you made in 1957--with your Bible under your arm in Time Magazine. I could make the commitment to answer my head and go forward, but not against your will and judgment. One of the reasons I cant \[*sic*\] think of commiting \[*sic*\] anymore of my life to this frustration is because I don’t believe in my heart that you and [[Helen Mattox Young|Helen]] would risk your esteem, your egos, your souls, your lives again in the commitment to save Pepperdine for the church. But I know that is _your_ deepest commitment and I cannot confront it.
>
>But I can say today something that I have never said before and that is, that if you will [[the opening of the board|open the Board]], I will commit to the open Pepperdine. I don’t think it means that we have to swing wildly to the left especially within the 5 to 10 years that you have left to work with it. I would think of adding dedicated business conservatives like [[Richard Scaife]], [[Carl Huntsinger]], [[Howard B. Keck, Jr.|Howard Keck]], [[Leonard K. Firestone|Leonard Firestone]], [[David Packard]], [[Richard Seaver]], [[John Payson|Johnny Payson]], [[Palmer Conner]], [[Miles Flint]] and [[Clint Murchison Jr.|Clint Muchison]] \[*sic*\]. They could join Bob Jones, [[George Evans]], [[Donald V. Miller|Don Miler]] \[*sic*\], you and Helen and Me, [[Lipscomb Crothers|Lip Crothers]], and all of the others who would like to stay. I can promise the following:
>1. I will stay as President of Pepperdine until you are 65 and I am 45.
>2. We will keep our basic political and business conservative approach for that period.
>C. That when we step aside the school will have an enormously better chance of survival than it will have if you take your course.
>D. We can’t work with an Institute, special workshops, etc., to keep many of the more enlightened brethren with us.
>
>What I can also promise is that the price of doing this will be twofold:
>3. In the short rance \[*sic*\] of the 7-year period there will be a greatly escalated tension with the brethren. I do not think it would be immediate secession, because in Texas and Tennessee we are already regarded as so far out that most of those who now tolerate us will continue to do so for this seven year period because of personal ties and efforts which can be made.
>4. But, in the longer run it will surely be secession--because most of what we disagree about is a matter of time--so sooner or later Pepperdine will either go under or else go open.
>
>I make these promises to you as genuinely as I can, on the basis of the good faith of our love and friendship. If you do not choose to accept me on these terms I must conclude that it is because you:
>5. Do not believe I am sincere
>6. Sincere, but will weaken and be drawn away before 7 years.
>7. Of \[*sic*\] that you prefer my departure to the increased short term hurt or heat?
>8. Or my departure to hastening the day when Pepperdine goes open.
>
>In any event, you should now help me by:
>9. Making the case that I have done my duty, paid my debts, given more than I have taken, tremendously helped the school into much stronger shape than it was in 1968 and should therefore be released.
>
>This is the best way with key insiders like [[Robert Jones|Bob]], [[George Evans|George]], [[Richard Seaver]], etc. If they get pessimistic and we fail to sell them, the erosion at the heart of the institution will be very serious.
>
>We must make peace, be optimistic, and begin to present [[Donald Sime|Don Sime]] as a successor who can carry on.
>
>We should _never_ again have another session like the one today. We’ll work past the executive committee and _all_ others must face this as a _fait accompli_, and catch our spirit of hope and confidence in the future. But I am _very_ confident that the school will make it. I think this decision has _more_ to do about the next 7 years of the school’s life than anything else. Beyond that, who can be certain? But my deep feeling is that by 1980 it will be dragged over the center line and be _more_ open than closed. and that will be that. Furthermore, I think in your departure the Board will go open to absorb the enormous loss of extraordinary and irreplacable \[*sic*\] administrative power and leadership, which is now alive, keeping it both economically viable and church-controlled.
>
>So in letting me go you are probably only sacrificing continued dynamic growth and physical and academic excellence _for_ consolidation, relative peace with the brethren, and a thrust which could do well in 7 years to pay the debt and operate in the black.
>
>And you will be sparing me a hard and lonely road. I’m prepared to bite the bullet. _You_ must or have decided which one I will bite.
