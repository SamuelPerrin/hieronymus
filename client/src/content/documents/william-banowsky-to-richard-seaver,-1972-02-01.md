This is a letter from Pepperdine president [[William S. Banowsky]] to donor [[Richard Seaver]], the nephew of [[Blanche Seaver]], dated February 1, 1972. In the letter, Banowsky expresses his warm regard for Seaver and assures him that his commitment to Pepperdine remains firm.

>February 1, 1972
>
>Dear [[Richard Seaver|Richard]]:
>For a long time now, I have wanted to share with you a few feelings from my heart. The quasi-formal nature of our friendship makes it difficult for me to say what I feel in our brief occasional chats. And knowing your heavy schedule of responsibilities makes me hesitant to take two hours from one of your busy days. Consequently, I have chosen to say it in this way.
>
>[[Gay Banowsky|Gay]] and I lived in Los Angeles from 1959 to 1963 while doing graduate work at [[USC]] and helping [[M. Norvel Young|Norvel]] at Pepperdine. But my principal work in those years was the ministry, and I zealously held forth for the little [[South Gate Church of Christ|church of Christ in South Gate]], where we lived. After finishing my doctorate, I was determined to preach the Word, and we accepted the very posh pulpit of a [[Broadway Church of Christ|great church in Lubbock, Texas]]. After five years, Norvel came to us with the tremendous challenge to move back to California to head up the establishment of Pepperdine’s [[Malibu campus|new campus]]. It was an agonizing decision to leave the ministry, doubly tough because it meant moving back to California, which neither Gay nor I preferred.
>
>That was 1968 and, at that time, I gave Norvel only a five-year commitment. Having been at Pepperdine before, I knew what an awesome challenge we were undertaking. I wasn’t certain we could make it so triumphantly, and Gay and I were not sure we wanted to rear our boys in Los Angeles. And beyond all of that, I also told Norvel at the time that I felt the tug of ambition for wider public service.
>
>But then is when God gave us [[Blanche Seaver|Mrs. Seaver]]. The Malibu site was chosen and everything started falling into place. Our little school was really going places. Frankly, Richard, I must pinch myself to believe that we’re really opening up this fall with such a tremendous plant and program.
>
>To make a long story short, Gay and I have signed up for five more years, because we can think of no better way to spend them. We’re looking forward to our [[Brock House|new house]] on the campus, and to all the rest. I feel a deep existential involvement with the campus, perhaps a sense of ownership, and we may never leave. I’d like to be a Mister Chips!
>
>On the other hand, I want to keep alive another option or two. [[Ronald Reagan|Governor Reagan]], [[David Packard]], [[Henry Salvatori]], [[Leonard K. Firestone|Leonard Firestone]], and some others interested in good government have urged me to help this year in Los Angeles County. Gay and I have given it deepest thought from all angles, and have talked to many people, including Mrs. Seaver. We’ve decided to do it. It’s for the [[Richard Nixon|President of the United States]], and I think he will be in office for another four years. He must carry California to win, and 40% of the State’s vote is in this County. Furthermore, he has a keen personal interest in carrying his home county. I have been reassured that my assignment will not involve raising _any_ money. Mr. Firestone is Finance Chairman. I sincerely believe that, properly handled, this service will be of direct and great benefit to our development program at Pepperdine. If I thought it would damage us, Richard, I’d pass. But, after all, we’re not [[Stanford|Stanford University]] -- yet! Like Avis we’ve got to try harder, and perhaps this kind of extraordinary involvement is just the ingredient to keep us on top with prospects and public attention. After talking to you first on the phone about this, I had a good, long visit with [[Glenn S. Dumke|Glenn Dumke]]. He explained that he had been a delegate to two Republican National Conventions. His rationale was that Norvel and I are already pretty well classified, and that we should make the most of it. “Exploit the opportunity to build you school into good financial shape,” he said, “and let your successors move it into top academic prestige. If you don’t do the first, they won’t get a chance to do the second.”
>
>I guess what I want you to know is that Norvel and [[Helen Mattox Young|Helen]] and [[Gay Banowsky|Gay]] and I feel the deepest moral obligation toward [[Frank R. Seaver|Mr.]] and Mrs. Seaver and [[Hydril Co.|Hydril Company]]. We are profoundly grateful for the role you have played in making Seaver College possible. Frankly, I’ve felt all along that Mrs. Seaver was getting the most marvelous blessing possible with this new campus; but I’m very much aware that we must work hard to _always_ make her proud of the blessing. It is my determination and hope that she will never be disappointed in any important matter.
>
>Actually, I’m now on board for a longer pull than ever. And I wanted to give you this larger perspective because otherwise the Republican thing might look to you like a departure. As you put it at the Navy Ball, “the beginning of the end.” But as you say, maybe I tend to take you too seriously. I’m working on that.
>
>But in reality, this is the beginning of Phase II for Gay and me at Pepperdine. It includes doing enough Republican work to keep alive an option. But there is no definite plan, and the timetable is long-range.
>
>As I said, I covet your respect and feel that the best basis for guys in bed as much as we are is to establish the clearest possible understanding.
>
>Since one of my priorities is to handle this assignment without any disappointment to Mrs. Seaver, any support you can lend will be helpful. I’ve explained to her that we’ve talked about it, and she was highly pleased at that. She prefers the point that I am doing this, not for Mr. Nixon personally, but because Governor Reagan has asked me, and for the good of the Republican cause.
>
>Finally, may I say that I feel the warmest affection for you personally. And not for the usual reasons. That is, above and beyond what you’ve done for Pepperdine. I’m grateful for your kind nature, your candor, and for the absence of greed and bigotry. Your sense of humor in the face of heavy responsibilities and unwillingness to take yourself too seriously are helpful lessons to intense young Turks like me. Norvel and I company with a lot of characters in this strange business of ours, and you rank high on our list of special human beings.
>
>Sincerely,
>\[unsigned\]
>William S. Banowsky
